Pick any of the 12 leads to see its strip (25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV) and the heart as that lead sees it. As the cursor sweeps across, the heart lights up to show which part is depolarizing or repolarizing, and the arrow shows why the trace goes up or down. The ECG Manual approach

Work every strip in this order. Normal values are the ones used in the uOttawa ECG Manual.

  1. RateCount-off method: 300, 150, 100, 75, 60, 50 per large box between R waves. Normal 60–100 bpm.
  2. RhythmRegular or irregular? Is there a P before every QRS and a QRS after every P? Normal sinus rhythm has both.
  3. AxisNormal −30° to +90°. Quick check: switch to leads I and aVF. Both mostly positive: normal. Lead I positive but aVF negative: check lead II; if II is positive (or equiphasic) the axis is still normal (0° to −30°), if II is negative it is left axis deviation.
  4. AlphabetP · PR 120–200 ms · Q · QRS < 100 ms (100–119 ms borderline; 120 ms or more is wide) · ST · T · QT (QTc 300–440/450 ms in the ECG Manual; in practice the main concern is a long QTc, above about 450 ms in men and 460 ms in women, and a very short QTc, under about 340 ms, is also abnormal)
